# Social Engineering Prevention

## Introduction

Social engineering attacks target people by exploiting trust, emotions, habits, and human behavior.

Because these attacks focus on people, technical security measures alone are not enough.

Security awareness and good security practices are important for reducing the risk of social engineering.

---

## Common Warning Signs

A suspicious request may involve:

* Unusual urgency
* Fear or threats
* Requests for confidential information
* Unexpected financial requests
* Requests for passwords
* Requests for authentication codes
* Suspicious links
* Unexpected attachments
* Unknown callers
* Unverified identities
* Requests to bypass normal procedures

---

## Think Before You Act

One of the most effective defenses is to stop and think before responding.

Ask yourself:

* Do I know who is contacting me?
* Was I expecting this message or call?
* Why is this person asking for this information?
* Is the request unusual?
* Can I verify the request independently?
* Is the message trying to make me act immediately?

Taking a few moments to verify a request can prevent a serious security incident.

---

## Verify Identities

Never assume that someone is legitimate simply because they claim to represent an organization.

When necessary, verify the person's identity through an independent and trusted communication channel.

For example, if someone claims to be from a bank, contact the bank using an official phone number or official application instead of using contact information provided by the suspicious message.

---

## Protect Sensitive Information

Sensitive information should not be shared unnecessarily.

This may include:

* Passwords
* Authentication codes
* Personal information
* Financial information
* Company information
* Confidential documents
* Security credentials

A legitimate organization should not normally require you to reveal your password.

---

## Use Strong Security Controls

Technical security controls can reduce the impact of successful social engineering attacks.

Useful measures include:

* Multi-factor authentication (MFA)
* Strong and unique passwords
* Password managers
* Regular software updates
* Antivirus and endpoint protection
* Email filtering
* Access controls
* Regular backups
* Network monitoring

---

## Security Awareness Training

Organizations should educate employees about social engineering.

Training can teach people how to recognize:

* Phishing
* Vishing
* Smishing
* Pretexting
* Baiting
* Tailgating

Regular awareness training can help employees recognize suspicious behavior before it becomes a security incident.

---

## What to Do If You Suspect an Attack

If you believe that you have encountered a social engineering attack:

1. Stop interacting with the attacker.
2. Do not provide additional information.
3. Do not click suspicious links.
4. Report the incident to the appropriate person or security team.
5. Change compromised passwords if necessary.
6. Monitor affected accounts.
7. Follow the organization's incident response procedures.

---

## Conclusion

Social engineering cannot be prevented by technology alone.

People must also understand how attackers manipulate trust, urgency, fear, curiosity, and authority.

The combination of security awareness, careful behavior, and technical security controls provides stronger protection against social engineering attacks.
