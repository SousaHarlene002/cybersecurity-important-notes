## Social Engineering ##

# What is Social Engineering?

## Introduction ##

Social engineering is a type of cyberattack that focuses on manipulating people rather than directly exploiting technical vulnerabilities.

Attackers use psychological manipulation, deception, and social interaction to convince people to perform an action or reveal information that should remain private.

Instead of trying to break into a system using only technical methods, an attacker may try to convince a person to provide a password, click on a malicious link, open an attachment, transfer money, or reveal sensitive information.

---

## Why is Social Engineering Dangerous? ##

Social engineering can be dangerous because people are an important part of every computer system and organization.

Even when an organization has strong technical security measures, an attacker may try to target an employee or user instead.

For example, an attacker could pretend to be:

* A bank employee
* A company manager
* A technical support agent
* A government organization
* A colleague
* A friend or family member

The attacker may use this false identity to gain the victim's trust and obtain sensitive information.

---

## How Does Social Engineering Work? ##

Social engineering attacks often rely on psychological techniques.

Attackers may try to create:

### Trust

The attacker pretends to be someone the victim knows or considers trustworthy.

### Urgency

The attacker creates a situation where the victim feels that they must act quickly.

For example:

> "Your account will be blocked today. Verify your information immediately."

### Fear

The attacker may threaten the victim with consequences if they do not follow the instructions.

### Curiosity

The attacker may use an interesting message, file, or link to encourage the victim to interact with it.

### Authority

The attacker may pretend to be someone with authority, such as a manager, bank employee, or system administrator.

---

## Common Types of Social Engineering ##

There are several common forms of social engineering.

### 1. Phishing

Phishing is an attack in which an attacker uses fake emails, websites, or messages to trick victims into revealing information or clicking on malicious links.

Phishing is one of the most common forms of social engineering.

### 2. Vishing

Vishing, or voice phishing, uses phone calls or other voice communication to deceive victims.

An attacker may pretend to be a bank employee, technical support agent, or another trusted person.

### 3. Smishing

Smishing is a form of phishing that uses SMS or text messages.

For example, a victim may receive a fake message claiming that a package could not be delivered and asking them to click on a link.

### 4. Pretexting

Pretexting occurs when an attacker creates a false story or situation to obtain information from a victim.

The attacker may invent a believable identity and reason for contacting the victim.

### 5. Baiting

Baiting uses something attractive or interesting to persuade a victim to take an action.

For example, an attacker might use a file, link, or physical device as bait.

### 6. Tailgating

Tailgating is a physical social engineering technique.

It occurs when an unauthorized person follows an authorized person into a restricted area without proper authorization.

---

## How Can People Protect Themselves? ##

Awareness is one of the most important defenses against social engineering.

Some useful security practices include:

* Do not share passwords or authentication codes with others.
* Be suspicious of unexpected messages and requests.
* Verify the identity of people requesting sensitive information.
* Avoid clicking on suspicious links.
* Check website addresses carefully before entering information.
* Do not allow unknown people to access restricted areas.
* Be careful with unexpected attachments.
* Use multi-factor authentication (MFA).
* Report suspicious messages or requests to the appropriate security team.

One important rule is:

**Think before you trust.**

If a message creates unusual urgency, fear, or pressure, take a moment to verify the situation before taking action.

---

## Social Engineering and Human Behavior ##

Social engineering demonstrates that cybersecurity is not only about technology.

Security also depends on human behavior.

A technically secure system can still be compromised if a person is manipulated into giving an attacker access or sensitive information.

For this reason, cybersecurity awareness and education are important parts of an organization's security strategy.

---

## Conclusion ##

Social engineering is a cybersecurity threat that exploits human trust, emotions, and behavior.

Instead of relying exclusively on technical vulnerabilities, attackers may manipulate people into performing actions that compromise systems or information.

Understanding social engineering is an important step toward recognizing suspicious situations and preventing successful attacks.
