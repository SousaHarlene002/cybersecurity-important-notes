# Pretexting

## Introduction

Pretexting is a social engineering technique in which an attacker creates a false story or situation, known as a **pretext**, to obtain information or convince a person to perform an action.

The attacker usually pretends to have a legitimate reason for requesting information.

---

## How Does Pretexting Work?

A pretexting attack generally involves:

1. Creating a believable scenario.
2. Pretending to be another person or organization.
3. Establishing credibility or trust.
4. Asking the victim for information or assistance.
5. Using the obtained information for malicious purposes.

The attacker may research the target beforehand to make the story more convincing.

---

## Examples of Pretexting

An attacker may pretend to be:

* A technical support employee
* A manager
* A bank employee
* A researcher
* A government official
* A coworker

For example, an attacker could contact an employee and claim to be from the company's IT department.

They might say that there is a problem with the employee's account and request information to "verify" their identity.

---

## Why Does Pretexting Work?

Pretexting often takes advantage of:

* Trust
* Authority
* Fear
* Urgency
* Helpfulness
* Lack of awareness

People may provide information because the request appears reasonable in the context of the false story.

---

## How to Protect Against Pretexting

To reduce the risk:

* Verify the identity of unexpected callers or visitors.
* Follow organizational security procedures.
* Do not provide sensitive information simply because someone claims to have authority.
* Use official communication channels for verification.
* Be cautious with unusual requests.
* Report suspicious behavior.

---

## Conclusion

Pretexting relies on believable stories and false identities to manipulate victims.

Learning to question unexpected requests and independently verify identities is an important defense against this type of social engineering.
