# Baiting

## Introduction

Baiting is a social engineering technique that uses something attractive, interesting, or tempting to persuade a person to take an action that may compromise their security.

The "bait" is designed to take advantage of human curiosity or desire.

---

## How Does Baiting Work?

An attacker creates something that the victim wants or finds interesting.

The victim interacts with the bait, and this may lead to:

* Malware infection
* Theft of information
* Unauthorized access
* Fraud
* Compromise of an account or device

---

## Examples of Baiting

### Digital Baiting

An attacker may offer:

* A free software download
* A fake prize
* A free file
* A tempting advertisement
* A fake promotion

The victim may download a malicious file or visit a dangerous website.

### Physical Baiting

Baiting can also involve physical devices.

For example, an unknown USB device may be left in a public or workplace location with the intention of making someone curious enough to connect it to a computer.

---

## Why Does Baiting Work?

Baiting takes advantage of human curiosity and expectations.

People may interact with something because they believe they are receiving a benefit.

---

## How to Protect Against Baiting

* Do not download software from unknown sources.
* Do not connect unknown USB devices to your computer.
* Be cautious with unexpected offers.
* Verify downloads through official sources.
* Use security software.
* Avoid files or links that seem suspicious.

---

## Conclusion

Baiting uses temptation and curiosity to encourage victims to take actions that may compromise their security.

Being cautious with unexpected files, devices, downloads, and offers can help prevent baiting attacks.
