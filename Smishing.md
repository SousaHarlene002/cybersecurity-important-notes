# Smishing

## Introduction

Smishing is a form of phishing that uses SMS or text messages to deceive victims.

The word "smishing" comes from the combination of **SMS** and **phishing**.

Attackers may send fraudulent messages containing malicious links, fake alerts, or requests for personal and financial information.

---

## How Does Smishing Work?

A smishing attack commonly works as follows:

1. The attacker sends a fraudulent text message.
2. The message appears to come from a legitimate organization or person.
3. The message creates curiosity, urgency, or fear.
4. The victim is encouraged to click a link or respond.
5. The attacker attempts to obtain information or compromise the victim's device or account.

---

## Common Smishing Examples

A fraudulent message may claim:

* A package could not be delivered.
* A bank account has a security problem.
* A payment failed.
* A prize has been won.
* An account needs verification.
* A subscription is about to expire.

For example:

> "Your package could not be delivered. Please confirm your address using the following link."

The message may contain a link leading to a fraudulent website.

---

## Warning Signs

Be careful when a text message:

* Comes from an unknown number.
* Contains a suspicious link.
* Requests passwords or financial information.
* Creates unnecessary urgency.
* Claims there is a serious problem with an account.
* Contains an unexpected attachment.
* Offers an unexpected prize or reward.

---

## How to Protect Against Smishing

Some useful practices include:

* Do not click suspicious links in text messages.
* Do not reply to suspicious messages.
* Verify the information through an official website or application.
* Never provide passwords or authentication codes through unexpected messages.
* Keep your phone and applications updated.
* Block or report suspicious numbers when appropriate.

---

## Conclusion

Smishing is a form of social engineering that uses text messages to manipulate victims.

Recognizing suspicious messages and verifying information through official channels are important steps in preventing smishing attacks.
