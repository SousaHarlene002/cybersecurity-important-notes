# Phishing

## Introduction

Phishing is one of the most common forms of social engineering.

It is a cyberattack in which an attacker pretends to be a trustworthy person, company, or organization in order to trick a victim into providing information, clicking on a malicious link, downloading a file, or performing another unwanted action.

Phishing attacks can target individuals, companies, schools, financial institutions, and government organizations.

---

## How Does Phishing Work?

A typical phishing attack follows several steps:

1. The attacker creates a fake message or communication.
2. The message is designed to appear legitimate.
3. The victim receives the message through email, a website, social media, or another communication channel.
4. The message encourages the victim to take an action.
5. The victim follows the instructions.
6. The attacker may obtain information, credentials, or access to an account.

The attacker often tries to create a sense of urgency or fear so that the victim does not have enough time to think about whether the message is legitimate.

---

## Common Phishing Techniques

### Fake Login Pages

An attacker may create a website that looks similar to a legitimate login page.

The victim enters their username and password, but the information is sent to the attacker instead of the legitimate service.

### Malicious Links

A phishing message may contain a link that appears legitimate but leads to a malicious or fraudulent website.

### Malicious Attachments

Some phishing emails contain attachments designed to trick the victim into opening them.

Attachments may contain malware or redirect the victim to a fraudulent website.

### Impersonation

The attacker may pretend to be a legitimate organization or person.

For example, they may impersonate:

* A bank
* A company
* A manager
* A delivery service
* A technical support team
* A government organization

---

## Types of Phishing

### Email Phishing

Email phishing uses fraudulent emails to deceive victims.

This is one of the most traditional and widespread forms of phishing.

### Spear Phishing

Spear phishing is a targeted phishing attack.

Instead of sending the same message to many people, the attacker creates a message specifically designed for a particular person or organization.

### Whaling

Whaling is a type of targeted phishing attack aimed at high-profile individuals, such as executives or senior managers.

### Clone Phishing

In clone phishing, an attacker creates a copy of a legitimate message and replaces legitimate links or attachments with malicious ones.

---

## Signs of a Phishing Attack

Some common warning signs include:

* Unexpected requests for passwords or personal information
* Messages creating a strong sense of urgency
* Suspicious email addresses
* Spelling or grammar mistakes
* Unexpected attachments
* Suspicious links
* Requests for financial information
* Offers that seem too good to be true
* Messages asking for authentication codes
* Websites with unusual or misspelled domain names

However, modern phishing attacks can look very professional, so spelling mistakes are not always present.

---

## How to Check a Suspicious Link

Before clicking a link, carefully examine its destination.

On a computer, you can often place the mouse pointer over the link without clicking it. This may show the destination address.

Look for:

* Misspelled domain names
* Unexpected domains
* Unusual characters
* Suspicious subdomains
* HTTP instead of HTTPS when sensitive information is requested

HTTPS provides encryption between the browser and the website, but HTTPS alone does **not** prove that a website is legitimate.

---

## How to Protect Against Phishing

Some important security practices include:

* Do not click suspicious links.
* Verify unexpected requests independently.
* Check the sender's email address carefully.
* Avoid opening unexpected attachments.
* Use multi-factor authentication.
* Use a password manager when appropriate.
* Keep software and operating systems updated.
* Report suspicious messages.
* Do not provide passwords or authentication codes through unexpected messages.

If a message appears to come from your bank, for example, do not use the telephone number or link provided in a suspicious message. Instead, contact the bank through an official channel.

---

## Example

Imagine receiving this message:

> "Your account has been suspended. Click here within 30 minutes to restore access."

This message uses **urgency** and **fear** to encourage the victim to act quickly.

Before clicking the link, the user should stop and verify the message through an official source.

---

## Conclusion

Phishing is a social engineering technique that uses deception to manipulate people into revealing information or performing actions that may compromise their security.

Learning to recognize suspicious messages, links, websites, and requests is an important part of protecting against phishing attacks.

Understanding phishing also provides a foundation for learning about other forms of social engineering, such as vishing and smishing.
