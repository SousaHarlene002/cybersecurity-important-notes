# Strong Passwords

## Introduction

Strong passwords are an important part of cybersecurity because they make it more difficult for attackers to gain unauthorized access to accounts and systems.

A strong password should be difficult to guess, unique to the account, and protected from unauthorized disclosure.

---

## What Makes a Password Strong?

Several characteristics can make a password more secure.

### Length

Longer passwords are generally more resistant to guessing attacks than short passwords.

A passphrase consisting of several unrelated words can provide both length and memorability.

### Uniqueness

A password should ideally be unique to one account.

Using the same password across multiple accounts increases the risk that a single compromised service could expose other accounts.

### Unpredictability

Passwords should avoid information that can easily be guessed, such as:

* Names
* Birthdays
* Phone numbers
* Addresses
* Favorite sports teams
* Common words
* Simple patterns

---

## Passwords vs. Passphrases

A password is not necessarily required to be a short combination of letters, numbers, and symbols.

A **passphrase** can consist of several words combined into a longer secret.

For example, instead of using a short and predictable password, a user can create a long passphrase that is easier to remember but difficult to guess.

The exact requirements depend on the service being used.

---

## Avoid Common Passwords

Attackers can use lists of commonly used passwords when attempting to access accounts.

Examples of weak passwords include:

```text
123456
password
qwerty
admin
welcome
```

Using common passwords makes automated guessing much easier.

---

## Avoid Personal Information

Personal information should not be used as the main basis of a password.

Information such as a person's name, birthday, pet's name, or favorite team may be discoverable through social media or other sources.

Attackers can sometimes use publicly available information to make their guesses more effective.

---

## Use a Different Password for Each Account

Using unique passwords limits the damage caused by a compromised account.

For example:

```text
Email → unique password
Social Media → unique password
Banking → unique password
School Account → unique password
```

If one password is exposed, the other accounts are not automatically protected by the same secret.

---

## Password Security Best Practices

* Use long and unique passwords.
* Avoid common passwords.
* Avoid predictable personal information.
* Do not reuse passwords between important accounts.
* Consider using a password manager.
* Enable multi-factor authentication whenever available.
* Never share passwords with other people.
* Avoid entering passwords on suspicious websites.

---

## Conclusion

Strong passwords reduce the likelihood of unauthorized access to accounts and systems.

Length, uniqueness, and unpredictability are important characteristics of secure passwords.

However, passwords are only one layer of protection. Combining strong passwords with password managers and multi-factor authentication can provide additional security.
