  #Introduction to Cybersecurity

## What is Cybersecurity? ##

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, networks, systems, applications, and data from unauthorized access, attacks, damage, or disruption.

As technology becomes an increasingly important part of our daily lives, cybersecurity plays an essential role in protecting both individuals and organizations from cyber threats.

Cybersecurity is not only about protecting computers from viruses. It involves a wide range of practices, technologies, and procedures designed to keep information and systems secure.

## Why is Cybersecurity Important? ##

Cybersecurity is important because computers and digital systems store and process large amounts of valuable information.

This can include:

* Personal information
* Passwords
* Financial information
* Business data
* Medical records
* Government information
* Communication and private files

If this information is stolen, modified, destroyed, or accessed without authorization, it can cause serious consequences for individuals and organizations.

Cybersecurity helps reduce these risks by protecting systems and information against different types of cyber threats.

## The Main Goals of Cybersecurity ##

One of the fundamental concepts in cybersecurity is the **CIA Triad**, which consists of three main principles:

### 1. Confidentiality

Confidentiality means ensuring that information can only be accessed by authorized people or systems.

For example, a password protects an account by preventing unauthorized users from accessing private information.

### 2. Integrity

Integrity means ensuring that information remains accurate and is not changed or manipulated without authorization.

For example, an attacker changing information in a database would be a violation of data integrity.

### 3. Availability

Availability means ensuring that systems and information are accessible when they are needed by authorized users.

For example, a website that becomes unavailable because of a cyberattack can affect the availability of that service.

## What is a Cyber Threat? ##

A cyber threat is a potential action, event, or situation that can negatively affect a computer system, network, or information.

Some common cyber threats include:

* Malware
* Phishing
* Social engineering
* Ransomware
* Password attacks
* Denial-of-Service (DoS) attacks
* Data breaches
* Unauthorized access

These threats can target individuals, companies, governments, and other organizations.

## Who Can Carry Out Cyberattacks? ##

Cyberattacks can be carried out by different types of threat actors.

Some examples include:

* Cybercriminals
* Hacktivists
* Insider threats
* Organized criminal groups
* Nation-state actors
* Security researchers and ethical hackers

Their motivations can vary from financial gain and political objectives to espionage, disruption, or security research.

## How Can Systems Be Protected? ##

Cybersecurity uses multiple layers of protection rather than relying on a single security measure.

Some common security practices include:

* Using strong and unique passwords
* Enabling multi-factor authentication (MFA)
* Keeping software and operating systems updated
* Using firewalls and security tools
* Encrypting sensitive information
* Creating regular backups
* Monitoring systems and networks
* Educating users about cyber threats
* Controlling access to sensitive information

## Cybersecurity is More Than Technology ##

An important aspect of cybersecurity is that not every attack depends on a technical vulnerability.

People can also be targeted through manipulation and deception.

For example, an attacker may send a fake email pretending to be a legitimate organization and attempt to convince someone to reveal their password.

This is known as **social engineering**, which will be explored in more detail in the next section of this repository.

## Conclusion ##

Cybersecurity is a broad field focused on protecting digital systems, networks, applications, and information.

Understanding the fundamentals of cybersecurity is an important first step toward learning how cyber threats work and how systems can be protected against them.
