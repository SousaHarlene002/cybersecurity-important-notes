# Vishing

## Introduction

Vishing is a form of social engineering that uses voice communication to deceive victims.

The word "vishing" comes from the combination of **voice** and **phishing**.

Instead of using email or text messages, attackers use phone calls or other voice-based communication to manipulate victims into revealing sensitive information or performing an action.

---

## How Does Vishing Work?

A typical vishing attack may follow these steps:

1. The attacker contacts the victim by phone.
2. The attacker pretends to represent a legitimate organization.
3. The attacker creates a believable situation.
4. The victim is asked to provide information or perform an action.
5. The attacker uses the information for malicious purposes.

The attacker may use urgency, fear, authority, or trust to influence the victim.

---

## Common Vishing Scenarios

An attacker may pretend to be:

* A bank employee
* A technical support agent
* A government representative
* A company employee
* A delivery company
* A telecommunications provider

For example, an attacker might claim that suspicious activity has been detected on the victim's bank account and request an authentication code.

---

## Warning Signs

Some warning signs include:

* Unexpected phone calls
* Requests for passwords
* Requests for authentication codes
* Requests for financial information
* Pressure to act immediately
* Threats of account suspension
* Requests to install software
* Requests for remote access to a computer
* Refusal to provide independent verification

---

## How to Protect Yourself

To reduce the risk of vishing:

* Never share passwords by phone.
* Never share authentication codes with unexpected callers.
* Do not allow unknown callers to remotely access your computer.
* Hang up if the situation seems suspicious.
* Contact the organization using an official phone number.
* Do not allow pressure or fear to force you into making a decision.
* Report suspicious calls when appropriate.

---

## Example

A person receives a phone call from someone claiming to be a bank employee.

The caller says:

> "We detected suspicious activity on your account. Please give me the verification code you just received."

The request should immediately raise suspicion.

A verification code is normally intended to confirm an action or login. Giving it to an attacker may allow them to complete an unauthorized action.

---

## Conclusion

Vishing uses voice communication and psychological manipulation to deceive victims.

Being cautious with unexpected phone calls and independently verifying the identity of callers can significantly reduce the risk of becoming a victim.
