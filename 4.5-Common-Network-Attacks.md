# Common Network Attacks

## Introduction

Network attacks are attempts to compromise, disrupt, monitor, or gain unauthorized access to systems and communications through a network.

Attackers can use different techniques depending on their objectives and the weaknesses they discover.

Understanding common network attacks is important for learning how networks can be protected.

---

## Denial-of-Service (DoS)

A Denial-of-Service attack attempts to make a system or service unavailable to legitimate users.

The attacker may overwhelm a target with excessive requests or traffic.

The main objective is usually to affect **availability**.

---

## Distributed Denial-of-Service (DDoS)

A Distributed Denial-of-Service attack is similar to a DoS attack, but the traffic comes from multiple systems or sources.

This can make the attack more difficult to mitigate.

```text
Device ─┐
Device ─┤
Device ─┼──→ Target
Device ─┤
Device ─┘
```

---

## Man-in-the-Middle (MitM)

A Man-in-the-Middle attack occurs when an attacker positions themselves between two communicating parties and attempts to intercept or manipulate their communication.

For example:

```text
User  ←→  Attacker  ←→  Server
```

Encryption and proper authentication can help protect communications against certain MitM attacks.

---

## Packet Sniffing

Packet sniffing involves capturing network packets for analysis.

Network monitoring tools can legitimately capture traffic for troubleshooting or security analysis.

However, attackers may also attempt to capture network traffic to obtain sensitive information.

Encrypted protocols can help protect the contents of network communications.

---

## Spoofing

Spoofing involves pretending to be another device, user, or service.

Examples include:

* IP spoofing
* DNS spoofing
* ARP spoofing
* Email spoofing

Spoofing techniques can be used as part of larger attacks.

---

## Network Scanning

Network scanning involves identifying hosts, ports, services, or other characteristics of a network.

Security professionals may use scanning for authorized security assessments.

Attackers may also perform scanning to identify potential targets and exposed services.

---

## Unauthorized Access

An attacker may attempt to gain access to a system through:

* Weak passwords
* Stolen credentials
* Vulnerabilities
* Misconfigured services
* Social engineering

Once access is obtained, the attacker may attempt to increase their privileges or access sensitive information.

---

## Malware

Malware can spread through networks and compromise connected systems.

Examples include:

* Viruses
* Worms
* Trojans
* Ransomware
* Spyware

Network segmentation, endpoint protection, access controls, and monitoring can help reduce the impact of malware.

---

## How to Protect Against Network Attacks

Organizations can use multiple layers of defense, including:

* Firewalls
* Network segmentation
* Strong authentication
* Encryption
* Security monitoring
* Regular updates
* Access controls
* Intrusion detection and prevention systems
* Secure network configuration
* Security awareness training
* Regular backups

No single security control can prevent every network attack.

---

## Conclusion

Network attacks can target the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of systems and information.

Understanding attacks such as DoS, DDoS, Man-in-the-Middle, spoofing, packet sniffing, and network scanning provides a foundation for learning defensive network security.

The most effective approach is to use multiple layers of security rather than relying on a single technology.
